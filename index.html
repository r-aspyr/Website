About

How I think

Why does someone choose one, and keep coming back?

In skincare, fashion and lifestyle, quality and good design have become the baseline. What separates the brands people stay with is harder to see, and that is what I try to work out.

I look at it from three positions, and at the dynamic between them: a brand sets a prediction, a consumer tests it, and the product confirms or breaks it. Tap a position.

When every brand makes the same promise, nobody registers the difference. The question in the middle is what sets the brand apart: what makes it hard to substitute?

From the brand: what it promises

Who is the brand speaking to, and what is their message? Does every touchpoint say the same thing?

From the consumer: how it is experienced

How do people discover, use and remember it, and where does the experience fall short of the promise?

From the product: what it proves

Does the product deliver on the promise, and what could it become, in materials, formats or adjacent territories, that only this brand could credibly make?

Selected work

From minimal to mindful

A brand audit of a botanical skincare brand. It starts from one observation: clean minimalism has become the category default, and for many brands calm has started to feel clinical.

The audit proposes sensory minimalism as a way for the brand to feel more human without losing its restraint. It maps the opportunity in three phases, from changes that can start now to longer-term moves.

This is not a commissioned project. It shows how I work: look closely, find the pattern, and turn it into options a team could act on.

Phase I · Immediate
  • Ritualized tactility
  • Material storytelling
  • Sound-led calm
Phase II · Low lift
  • Material depth
  • Verbal tone
  • Atmospheric world
Phase III · Long term
  • Retail ritual
  • Slow digital touchpoints
  • Collaborative calm
